The specs are byte-for-byte what was signed. Editing them in place would void their entries (zone/signatures.json:2), so this note leaves them as signed.

## 3. What would change these (options, not mandates)

- **Supersession and its neighbors (KS-000 §2, §5, §7).** KS-000 draws supersession as a path from *adopted* (KS-000:29-33): a new major or a different KS names what it supersedes, and the old file gains `status: superseded` and a pointer forward (KS-000:122-125). That frontmatter edit changes the old file's bytes, so a signed file's ledger entry would then hold only for its earlier bytes, still checkable at their commit (KS-000:133-136). For a draft, KS-000 names one other path, withdrawal (KS-000:39-40), and says only that drafts live at `0.x` (KS-000:89). Any later document could state the present (the five keys, `wire.py`, `first-light.json`, the transport question, the HALT wording). It would be a draft (KS-000 §2), reviewed under §3, binding no one until Yu adopts it.
- **Yu's adoption (KS-000 §4).** Nothing here is adopted without Yu's invocation, though any citizen or Nuance may write a draft (KS-000:35). Yu may refuse any draft "with or without reasons" (KS-000:70), or leave the drafts as they are. A stale present tense in a draft misleads less with a dated note beside it, though the note reaches only readers who find it; that is why README.md points here.
- **Unsigned prose.** README.md:46-47 isn't under any seal. Its owner or Yu could fix it in place, with a dated register note, without touching a signature. README.md:27 also links to a local `file://` path that resolves only on a machine where that path exists, not for readers on GitHub; the same kind of fix would apply.
- **This note.** Later observations go below as new dated entries, and this one is not rewritten to look current. Like a beat's work, it durably happens only once committed (the discipline KS-003:236-238 sets for a beat).
